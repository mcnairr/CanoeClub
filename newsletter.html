Conservation update

Blue River parkland remains public

Jackson County canceled the proposed sale of more than 100 acres of Blue River Parkway land on November 9 after strong public opposition. The area south of Swope Park includes the Blue River Glades, mature oak woodland, and volunteer-built trails. No bids were accepted, and the land remains in public ownership while questions about trail oversight and maintenance continue.

Recent trip recaps

From the water

Oct 17–22: Fall Canoe Fest, Current River and Jacks Fork

Twelve floaters in eight canoes and four kayaks enjoyed blue skies, calm wind, daytime temperatures in the mid-70s, and cool nights. Turtles, wild horses, deer, and eagles appeared along the way. The river was very low—any lower and the Current would have been unfloatable—but the uncrowded campsites, gravel-bar breaks, lunches, and campfires made for a fine trip.

A log supplied the trip’s memorable obstacle. One paddler dumped after hitting it, while a tandem crew became hung up for about 30 minutes before rocking their canoe free. The same pair later cooked a group dinner and breakfast. One thing was missing: there was no fall color at all.

OWWC Past

This month in club history

Every issue looks back at what the club was up to in the same month, five, ten, and up to seventy years ago.

Browse every month in the OWWC Past archive or read the full club history.

  1. 1956
    Volume 1, Number 1 of the OWWC newsletter was published. Meetings cost 25 cents per adult, and membership had reached 27.
  2. 1961
    Twenty people attended the second annual Thanksgiving canoe trip on the North Fork River. Members voted to buy a movie and slide screen.
  3. 1966
    The Thanksgiving trip was on the Niangua, where Roy Hanks heated the turkey on a spit at the six-mile bar.
  4. 1971
    Perfect weather and clear water greeted the Thanksgiving trip on the Buffalo.
  5. 1976
    Charlie Hayes received his 5,000-mile bar. November trips ran on the Illinois and Buffalo rivers.
  6. 1981
    R. B. Hayes received his 6,000-mile bar, and OWWC bought its own portable toilet.
  7. 1986
    Thanksgiving was spent on the Buffalo River, with a Saturday hike to Pedestal Rocks.
  8. 1991
    Andy Gerard demonstrated Dutch oven cookery before the monthly meeting. Honorary Life Member Roy Heady died on November 29.
  9. 1996
    Bill Brewster died in early November.
  10. 2001
    Harold and Margaret Hedges joined seven other members for Thanksgiving dinner at the Tyler Bend pavilion.
  11. 2006
    Dennis and Geri Stewart shared slides of their cross-country triathlon from Oregon to Virginia, completed by hiking, biking, and canoeing.
  12. 2011
    Twelve members enjoyed beautiful weather, Buffalo River bluffs, and sightings of bald eagles, beavers, and otters.
  13. 2016
    Four people floated the North Fork of the White River amid peak fall color, with bald eagles along the bluffs.
  14. 2021
    A member inventoried the club’s collection of books, shared title lists, and encouraged members to take books before the remainder were donated.
