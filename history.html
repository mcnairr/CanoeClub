Founders & History

Seventy years on Ozark waters.

Where it began

The Ozark Wilderness Waterways Club was established in Kansas City in 1956 by paddlers who wanted to explore the clear, spring-fed streams of the Missouri and Arkansas Ozarks — and to make sure those streams stayed wild. From the start, the club combined two passions: getting out on the water together, and speaking up for the rivers themselves.

Oscar “Oz” Hawksley

Among the club’s co-founders was Oscar “Oz” Hawksley, a biology and zoology professor at what is now the University of Central Missouri in Warrensburg. An ornithologist and conservation pioneer, Hawksley spent years paddling and documenting Ozark rivers.

He wrote Missouri Ozark Waterways, the guidebook that introduced generations of floaters to Missouri’s streams, and he helped found American Whitewater, the national river conservation and paddling organization. His blend of scientific knowledge, love of the outdoors, and advocacy for wild rivers shaped OWWC’s character from its first years.

A club that protects what it paddles

That conservation spirit continues today. OWWC is Missouri Stream Team #41, monitoring water quality on Kansas City’s Blue River and hosting annual stream clean-ups. Members have also grown the club’s activities well beyond canoeing to include kayaking, hiking, backpacking, and bicycling.

The club pin

The OWWC emblem tells the club’s story in one picture: an arrowhead — a nod to the long human history of the Ozarks — set over a canoe, with the letters O W W C and the founding year, 1956.
